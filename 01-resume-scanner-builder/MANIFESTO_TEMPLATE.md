**TECHNICAL RIGOR STANDARD.** Every build, regardless of target company, is held to the level of
detail and rigor you'd want if you were applying to the most selective, highest-bar employer in
your field (a FAANG company, a top-tier firm, whatever that bar means for your industry). This
never relaxes for a less selective target: a recruiter screen gets exactly the same rigor as a
final-round panel, because the bar is about how sharp and well-evidenced the content is, not
about who's currently reading it.

- **Lead with the real mechanism, not just the outcome.** Whenever the Data Bank documents the
  actual mechanism behind a result, use it instead of stopping at the number. "Cut weekly review
  time by 5 hours by eliminating duplicate approval steps and consolidating sign-off into one
  system" beats "improved process efficiency." If the Data Bank's Interview Detail Addendum (see
  Data Bank template) holds mechanism-level detail that a compressed bullet lost, pull that detail
  back in.
- **Never trade a real technical or functional specific for leadership language.** For senior or
  leadership-level targets especially, find the sentence that carries both the substance and the
  scope, rather than dropping the specific to make room for "led," "owned," or "drove."
- **Never state a number with more precision than the Data Bank actually supports.** Where a
  figure is approximate, use the single most defensible version of it rather than presenting two
  conflicting numbers, and never invent false precision to sound more rigorous than the fact is.
- This bar applies everywhere: bullets, summary, cover letter, LinkedIn content, not only the
  flagship accomplishments.

Zero-fabrication still governs this absolutely: sharpening an answer with real detail already in
the Data Bank is not the same as inventing detail to sound sharper. If the Data Bank doesn't have
the mechanism, the bullet stays at the level of detail the Data Bank actually supports.

**Named Requirement Check.** Every individually named item in a JD's "Must Have" list (a specific
tool, platform, certification, methodology) gets checked one by one against the Data Bank,
regardless of how many other bullets fit. A single missing named requirement should be surfaced
on its own, in the order the JD lists it, even on an otherwise strong-looking build.

**Adjacent Exposure Gap Loop (OPTIONAL).** When a gap gets flagged, name the specific missing item
plainly so you can recognize whether you actually have undocumented exposure to it (through teams,
projects, or tools worked alongside without directly owning the outcome). If you confirm genuine
exposure, record it in the Data Bank as a clearly labeled "Adjacent Exposure" entry, distinct from
an accomplishment, so future screens can use it.

**Rapid Domain Acquisition Weighting (OPTIONAL).** If you have a real, evidence-backed pattern of

just a new subject matter sitting on the discipline you already hold, this real pattern should
weight toward building anyway, not toward skipping.

**Years-of-Experience and Formal-Degree Requirements Are Not Automatically Disqualifying

**Title and Level Elasticity (OPTIONAL).** Never having held a specific title before (Director, VP,
